Dot Products

From 3Blue1Brown, Essence of Linear Algebra, Chapter 9
Linear Algebra Lesson 09
Concept

Measuring How Much Two Vectors Agree

The dot product takes two vectors of the same length and produces a single number. That number tells you how much the two vectors point in the same direction. It is one of the most important operations in all of linear algebra, and it shows up constantly in machine learning.

The core idea: multiply corresponding components and add them up. \(\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = v_1 w_1 + v_2 w_2 + \cdots + v_n w_n\)

What the sign tells you

The dot product is positive when the two vectors point in roughly the same direction, zero when they are perpendicular, and negative when they point in roughly opposite directions. This is the geometric interpretation, and it is exactly what you need to understand attention.

Projection

There is a deeper geometric meaning. The dot product of \(\mathbf{v}\) with a unit vector \(\hat{u}\) gives you the length of the shadow that \(\mathbf{v}\) casts onto \(\hat{u}\). This is called the projection of \(\mathbf{v}\) onto \(\hat{u}\). Multiply that length by \(\hat{u}\) and you get the projection vector, which is the component of \(\mathbf{v}\) that lies along \(\hat{u}\).

Connection to matrix multiplication

A dot product is also a special case of matrix multiplication. If you write \(\mathbf{v}\) as a row vector and \(\mathbf{w}\) as a column vector, then \(\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{v}^T \mathbf{w}\). This is why the dot product is sometimes called an inner product, and it is how matrix-vector multiplication is computed row by row.

Formulas

Key Formulas

Algebraic definition
\[ \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} v_i w_i \]
Geometric definition
\[ \mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w} = \|\mathbf{v}\| \, \|\mathbf{w}\| \cos\theta \]
Projection length onto unit vector
\[ \text{proj length} = \mathbf{v} \cdot \hat{u} \]
Projection vector
\[ \text{proj}_{\hat{u}} \mathbf{v} = (\mathbf{v} \cdot \hat{u}) \, \hat{u} \]

Code Examples

Part 1: Numerical dot product

Computing the dot product manually by multiplying components and summing, then verifying with NumPy.

Python lesson-09-dot-products.py 
import numpy as np

v1 = np.array([1, 2])
v2 = np.array([4, -5])

dot_prod = v1[0] * v2[0] + v1[1] * v2[1]

numpy_dot = np.dot(v1, v2)

print(f"Manual: {dot_prod}")
print(f"NumPy: {numpy_dot}")
Output
Manual: -6
NumPy: -6

Part 2: Geometric interpretation

Verifying the sign rule: same direction gives positive, perpendicular gives zero, opposing gives negative.

Python lesson-09-dot-products.py 
# similar direction, dot product should be positive
v1 = np.array([1, 2])
v2 = np.array([2, 3])
print(f"Similar direction: {np.dot(v1, v2)}")

# perpendicular, dot product should be zero
v3 = np.array([3, 4])
v4 = np.array([4, -3])
print(f"Perpendicular: {np.dot(v3, v4)}")

# opposing direction, dot product should be negative
v5 = np.array([1, 2])
v6 = np.array([-2, -9])
print(f"Opposing Directions: {np.dot(v5, v6)}")
Output
Similar direction: 8
Perpendicular: 0
Opposing Directions: -20

Part 3: Projection

Dotting a vector with a unit vector gives the projection length directly. Multiplying that length by the unit vector gives the projection vector.

Python lesson-09-dot-products.py 
v = np.array([3, 4])
u = np.array([1, 0])  # unit vector pointing along x-axis

print(f"Length of u: {np.linalg.norm(u)}")

# the dot product with a unit vector gives you the projection length directly
projection_length = np.dot(v, u)
print(f"Projection of v onto u: {projection_length}")

# the actual projection vector is the length times the unit vector
projection_vector = projection_length * u
print(f"Projection vector: {projection_vector}")
Output
Length of u: 1.0
Projection of v onto u: 3
Projection vector: [3 0]
AI Connection

How This Connects to AI

Why this matters for machine learning

The dot product is the single most important operation in transformer models. Attention asks one question: "how relevant is this token to that token?" It answers that question by computing a dot product between their vector representations. A high dot product means the two tokens are highly relevant to each other. A low or negative dot product means they are not.

In the attention mechanism, every token is represented by a query vector and a key vector. The attention score between two tokens is the dot product of a query with a key: \(\text{score} = \mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{k}\). These scores are then passed through a softmax to produce weights, and those weights determine how much each value vector contributes to the output. The dot product is the entire mechanism by which a transformer decides what to pay attention to.

The same idea appears in cosine similarity, which is just a normalized dot product. When you search for similar documents or similar embeddings in a vector database, you are computing dot products. When a language model retrieves relevant context in RAG, it is comparing dot products between query embeddings and document embeddings.

Projection also matters directly. In PCA and dimensionality reduction, you project high-dimensional data onto a lower-dimensional subspace using dot products. The projection captures the most important components of the data while discarding the rest. This is how you compress a 4096D embedding down to something smaller without losing too much information.
